# Bunny English — Lesson Quality Guide (A0 and future levels)

This release deliberately postpones runtime AI/API tutoring. The quality bar must be met by the lesson itself.

## Core teaching order

1. **Meaning first** — learner understands what the sentence is trying to say.
2. **Notice** — learner compares examples and spots a change.
3. **Simple explanation in natural Vietnamese** — short, conversational, adult-friendly.
4. **Grammar name later** — only after the idea already makes sense.
5. **Hear** — natural reference audio.
6. **Say** — optional; learner may use “Tôi không thể nói lúc này”.
7. **Build** — learner reconstructs the sentence.
8. **Produce** — learner creates their own English.
9. **Review** — mistakes return later through the review queue.

## Vietnamese writing standard

Write like an experienced Vietnamese English teacher speaking to an adult learner.

Prefer:
- “Nhìn hai câu này nhé.”
- “Bạn thấy từ nào thay đổi?”
- “Trong tiếng Việt mình nói…, còn tiếng Anh cần…”
- “Chưa cần nhớ tên ngữ pháp ngay.”
- “Thử lại và nhìn vào phần cuối của động từ.”

Avoid:
- literal translation from English teaching materials;
- unnecessary English jargon inside Vietnamese explanations;
- long academic definitions before examples;
- childish praise or patronizing language;
- technical product language such as “system heard”, “production step”, or “mastery credit”.

## Bilingual rule

English examples and Vietnamese explanations do **not** need to mirror each other word-for-word.
The Vietnamese should explain the English in the most natural way for a Vietnamese learner.

Example:

`She is tired.`

Natural teaching:
> “She is tired.” = “Cô ấy mệt.” Trong tiếng Việt không cần một từ tương đương với **is** ở giữa. Trong tiếng Anh thì cần: **She is tired.**

Not:
> “Cô ấy là mệt.”

## Terminology rule

Use this progression:

`Câu đang nói về ai/cái gì?` → `chủ ngữ` → `Subject` → `S`

`Người/vật đó làm gì?` → `động từ` → `Verb` → `V`

Do not start with S/V/O/C before the learner understands the human question behind each label.

## Adult learner rule

Simple does not mean childish. Assume the learner may be 18, 40, 60, or older.
Use calm, respectful language and one idea at a time.
